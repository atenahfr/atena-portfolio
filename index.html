About

I'm a Computer Science student at Toronto Metropolitan University who ended up in security almost by accident. I was capturing routine network traffic during a research project and noticed something that shouldn't have been readable. It was. That one thirty-minute discovery, a live cryptography experiment leaking secrets in plaintext over a mesh network, is basically how I think about most systems now: the math can be perfect and the whole thing can still fail somewhere nobody was looking.

Most of what I build lives in that gap. I've led a team of five through two agile sprints to ship a full Scrum management tool, written a log analysis dashboard that flags brute-force patterns and anomalies in server logs, and dug into the distance between what people say they want protected and what they actually let slip, especially around location data. Right now I'm part of a team building a Rust tool that splits files across cloud providers so no single one ever holds enough to reconstruct them, its own small bet that trust should be distributed, not assumed.

I'm looking for the next place to point that curiosity, ideally somewhere doing real security or privacy research.
